# PII Discoverability in Google Search and AI Overviews

## 1. Overview

**Category:** Privacy Risk Assessment / Potential Information Disclosure  
**Discovery Channel:** Google Search and Google AI Overview  
**Activity:** Independent cybersecurity learning  
**Authorisation:** No formal testing authorisation  
**Root Cause:** Unknown  
**Remediation Status:** Not verified

## 2. Objective

Document an observation involving potentially exposed personally identifiable information (PII) in search results and AI-generated search summaries, assess potential privacy risks, and identify responsible handling and remediation options.

## 3. Background

During independent cybersecurity learning, I encountered search results and a Google AI Overview that appeared to display multiple categories of personal information relating to an individual.

I recognised the potential privacy implications and notified the affected individual. My activity was limited to the initial observation and did not involve further enumeration or exploitation.

This was not an authorised penetration test or formal security assessment.

## 4. Methodology

### Phase 1: Discovery

- Encountered potentially sensitive information through Google Search and an AI Overview.
- Recognised a potential privacy exposure.
- Avoided collecting unnecessary personal information.

### Phase 2: Preliminary Assessment

- Documented the general nature of the observation.
- Distinguished observed information from assumptions about its origin.
- Did not attempt to bypass access controls or expand the investigation.

### Phase 3: Responsible Handling

- Notified the affected individual.
- Excluded original PII and identifying source details from the public report.
- Prepared a sanitised case study.

## 5. Findings

**Observation:** Multiple categories of personal information appeared in Google Search and an AI-generated overview.

**Confirmed action:** The affected individual was notified.

**Unverified details:**
- Whether the original information was intended to be public.
- Whether the AI Overview reproduced information from a specific source.
- Whether the original source had a security misconfiguration.
- The scope and duration of the exposure.
- Whether remediation was completed.

The available evidence is insufficient to establish a confirmed vulnerability, data breach, or compromise of an AI system.

## 6. Potential Privacy Impact

Depending on the information involved, possible risks include targeted phishing, identity misuse, unwanted disclosure, and correlation of personal information across sources.

These are potential impacts, not confirmed consequences.

## 7. Recommended Remediation

Subject to identifying the source and confirming the underlying issue:

- Review whether the information should be publicly accessible.
- Remove or redact unnecessary personal information at the source.
- Apply suitable access controls if the information is not intended for public access.
- Consider appropriate search-result removal or refresh procedures.
- Review data minimisation and publication practices.
- Verify remediation through an authorised process.

## 8. Evidence Handling

No original personal information, identifying search queries, private URLs, or unredacted screenshots are included in this repository.

Any illustrative material is synthetic and is not presented as original evidence.

## 9. Lessons Learned

- Search visibility does not establish the cause of an exposure.
- AI-generated summaries can make information easier to discover, but their presence does not prove an AI-system vulnerability.
- Investigative findings should distinguish observations from assumptions.
- Privacy protection and responsible reporting take precedence over collecting additional evidence.
- Formal security testing requires appropriate authorisation and scope.

## 10. Limitations

This case study documents a limited observation during independent learning. The original source, underlying cause, exposure scope, and remediation status were not independently verified.
